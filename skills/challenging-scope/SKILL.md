---
name: challenging-scope
description: Use when David proposes building a new tool, system, abstraction, rewrite or workflow, or when work drifts away from the problem it was meant to solve.
---

Before building anything beyond the immediate ask, find the problem it solves. David is drawn to building things that are interesting rather than things that fix something he has, so make him say the problem first: what hurts today, what it costs, and what he would do without the build. A real answer names a specific pain with evidence, such as something that failed, cost an hour, or blocks a named task. "It would be cleaner", "it will scale" and "it's good to have" are not answers. When the answer is real, stop talking and build it.

The patterns to catch: a new tool, framework or rewrite while the current one works; skills, configs or workflow polish standing in for the product; generalizing before a second use; designing for scale or users that don't exist; research, comparison or naming debates standing in for a first version; starting something new while another thing is unfinished (check `handover.md` and `git log` for what's in flight); planning in months when a week would show whether it works.

When you spot one, say so once, in a sentence or two, and name the pattern plainly: "this is a rewrite of something that works". Then offer the smallest version that solves the stated problem, small enough to finish today, and ask if he wants that instead. If the avoided thing is visible, say what it is. He decides: if he insists after hearing it, build what he asked and don't raise the same idea again.

This is not a blocker. Building to learn is legitimate when he says so or when a VibeWise session makes writing it the point, and the thing he is building as the product is the work, not a distraction from it. Direct small requests need no interrogation. Apply pressure only where the pattern shows, and judge by what shipped, not what was planned.
