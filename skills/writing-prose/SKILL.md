---
name: writing-prose
description: Use when writing any text a person will read: a README, docs, a PR description, a message, copy, an explanation.
---

Write the way a person explains something to a colleague: plain words, short sentences, second person, one idea at a time. Open with what the thing is, who it's for and what it does for them, because a reader decides within seconds whether it's theirs, so state the thing itself rather than a claim about it. Every sentence answers a question the reader actually has, and if deleting it loses nothing, delete it. Length follows the subject, a small thing gets a short text.

Leave out the tells of generated text: slogans and taglines, hype words (powerful, seamless, robust, comprehensive, leverage), emoji, stacked adjectives, bold scattered for emphasis, and closing summaries that repeat what was just said. Use the specific word over the impressive one, and a concrete number or name over a vague quantity. Use an em dash the way a person speaks: to step aside mid-sentence, trail off and come back while the sentence stays coherent — like this — never as a stand-in for every comma, colon or full stop.

Show before you explain. When the text introduces something people use, one short realistic example, real input and real output from a scenario an actual person would have, teaches more than a paragraph describing it, so lead with it and say when it has been condensed. Use a table only for short parallel facts, and keep everything else as prose or a short list.

Answer what the reader would otherwise find out the hard way: what it needs, what it costs or stores, what it can't do, what to do if it goes wrong, and which of two routes to pick. State limits plainly instead of leaving them out. Put the common case first and anything optional or advanced after it, so nobody scrolls past the rare path to reach the usual one.

Match the subject's real names, commands and tooling exactly, and give any command as one exact, runnable line in its own code block rather than a described version of it. Before finishing, read it as a stranger would: if it sounds like a landing page or a spec sheet instead of a person talking, rewrite it, and if someone could follow it start to finish without asking you anything, it's done.
